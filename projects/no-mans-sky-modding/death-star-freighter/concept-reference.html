Death Star Freighter

Concept reference for reskinning Mothership — the player's existing S-class capital freighter — as an original spherical battle-station hull. Design only: nothing built, nothing installed.

Target: Mothership Class: S (already owned) Donor hull: Venator-family wedge Stat/class edits: none planned

Silhouette concept

The sphere is the whole silhouette — everything else is detail on top of it. Two placement rules keep the surface from reading as a plain ball: the dish sits off the pole and off the equator, and the trench runs full circumference at the midline.

The non-negotiables

Judged from a distance, before any surface detail is visible. If a build gets these five wrong, no amount of greebling fixes it.

  1. 01

    Near-perfect sphere

    Every stock NMS freighter hull is elongated. A sphere is the one silhouette break nothing else in the base game has — don't let it drift toward an ellipsoid or a flattened disc.

  2. 02

    One offset dish, concave

    Crater-like, never domed. Off the equator, off the pole. A centered or convex dish is the single most common mistake in fan reproductions.

  3. 03

    Continuous equatorial trench

    A shallow canyon at the full circumference, denser greebling inside it than on open plating. Splits the sphere into hemispheres without breaking the silhouette.

  4. 04

    Dense, uniform paneling

    A fine grid across the whole sphere. This is what reads as "Death Star" instead of "moon" at range — avoid large blank stretches of hull.

  5. 05

    No forward/aft asymmetry

    This is a station, not a ship with a nose. Orientation follows wherever Mothership's stock hangar mouth already sits — the sphere doesn't get to impose one.

Fitting it over Mothership

Mothership's confirmed hull is a long, flat, angular-bowed wedge — a Venator-family silhouette, not a rounder one. A sphere can't hug that shape; it has to fully enclose it. The envelope rule: sphere diameter = the hull's full length, the largest dimension there is to work with. Capital freighters are commonly estimated around 200–400m long (community consensus, not an official spec) — so that's roughly the sphere's real-world scale, going by this rule.

The hangar sits inside the equatorial trench, not centered under the dish. It's the more canonical placement — the trench, not the dish, is where the source material puts its landing bays — and the easier one to build safely: the trench is a full ring at one height, so only that one height has to line up with the real hangar mouth. Centering it under the dish would mean matching an exact point in two directions, and cutting a flight path through the middle of a concave crater risks the opening's edges brushing the crater's own geometry.

Surface & materials

Cold gunmetal hull, darker trench and dish interior, warm accent reserved for the dish well only — the opposite of the sibling Falcon Corvette project's blue engine-strip glow.
